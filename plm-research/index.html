Hypothesis

Ankh outperforms larger models on contact prediction

Initial claim: Ankh achieves 49% precision on ProteinNet L/1, beating ProtT5-XL-U50 (44.7%) and ESM-2 15B (33.3%) despite fewer parameters.

▼
Evidence

Ankh Table 13 reports 49.0% ±8% for Ankh

Source: Ankh paper Table 13, extracted as "49.0% Contact Prediction ProteinNet L/1 precision Ankh". Appears to support the hypothesis.

▼
Contradiction

Source data flagged: "Table row text truncated in extraction"

The values.csv entry for this metric carries status="flagged" with note: "Table row text truncated in extraction; value 49.0 present in table". The Ankh paper itself may not have run all models under identical conditions — Table 13 may compile results from different experimental setups.

▼
Revision

Withdraw comparative claim; treat as unverified

Cannot conclude Ankh > ProtT5 > ESM-2 for contact prediction. The 49% figure may be valid for Ankh alone, but cross-model comparison requires: (1) same benchmark split, (2) same evaluation protocol, (3) same sequence length limits. None verified. Flagged data must be excluded from comparative claims.

Lesson: Benchmark tables in papers often aggregate results from heterogeneous sources. Always verify: same test set? same metric definition? same preprocessing? If extraction is flagged or provenance unclear, treat as hypothesis-generating only — not evidence for model ranking.

Novel Falsifiable Hypothesis & Proposed Experiment

One testable research direction emerging from the evidence review.

Hypothesis (not a demonstrated result)

"Intermediate-layer protein embeddings may recover functional relationships among divergent microbial proteins more effectively than final-layer embeddings, under matched evaluation conditions."

Rationale: Final layers in PLMs are optimized for the training objective (masked language modeling, next-token prediction). Intermediate layers may retain more generalizable evolutionary and structural signals — particularly for distant homologs where surface-level sequence similarity is low but functional constraints persist.

Proposed Experiment

Compare embedding layers and pooling strategies against sequence-based baselines on a curated microbial protein function benchmark.

Models

  • ESM-2 (650M, 3B, 15B)
  • All transformer layers

Layer Selection

  • Final layer (baseline)
  • Middle layer (e.g., layer 12/33)
  • Early layer (e.g., layer 4/33)
  • Layer-wise sweep

Pooling

  • Mean pooling
  • Max pooling
  • CLS / first token
  • Attention-weighted

Sequence Baselines

  • BLASTP (E-value, %id)
  • MMseqs2 (bit score)
  • Profile-HMM (HMMER3)

Evaluation

  • Microbial protein families
  • Divergent pairs (<30% id)
  • Function labels: EC, GO, Pfam
  • Metrics: AUPRC, recall@k

Controls

  • Matched sequence length
  • Same test pairs for all methods
  • Random embedding baseline
  • Layer-shuffled control
Falsification criterion: If final-layer mean-pooled embeddings match or exceed all intermediate-layer/pooling combinations on AUPRC for divergent microbial protein pairs (≤30% sequence identity), the hypothesis is falsified.
Scope: This is a proposed experiment, not a result. No PLM layer-comparison study on divergent microbial proteins with these controls exists in the reviewed literature. The hypothesis is falsifiable and specific enough to preregister.
