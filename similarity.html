Lesson 3: Cosine Similarity — Measuring Protein Relatedness

📚 For beginner bioinformatics students | ⏱️ ~10 minutes

The Question: How Similar Are Two Proteins?

We now have protein embeddings — fixed-size vectors representing entire proteins. But how do we compare them? We need a similarity metric.

The most common choice in protein language models is cosine similarity.

What Is Cosine Similarity?

Cosine similarity measures the cosine of the angle between two vectors:

cosine_similarity(A, B) = (A · B) / (||A|| × ||B||)
           = Σ(Aᵢ × Bᵢ) / (√ΣAᵢ² × √ΣBᵢ²)

# Range: -1 to 1
#  1.0 = identical direction (same protein)
#  0.0 = orthogonal (unrelated)
# -1.0 = opposite direction (rare in practice)

Key property: Cosine similarity ignores vector magnitude (length) and only cares about direction. This is perfect for embeddings because we care about the pattern of the protein, not how "strongly" it's expressed.

Why Cosine, Not Euclidean Distance?

  • Scale-invariant: Multiplying an embedding by 2 doesn't change cosine similarity
  • High-dimensional friendly: In high dimensions, Euclidean distances become less discriminative
  • Standard in NLP/pLMs: Word/protein embeddings are typically normalized to unit length
  • Interpretable: 0.9 = very similar, 0.5 = moderate, 0.1 = dissimilar
Practical note: Most protein language models (ESM, ProtBERT) output embeddings that are already normalized or work best with cosine similarity. Always check the model's documentation!

Real-World Applications

Cosine similarity on protein embeddings powers many bioinformatics tasks:

  • Homology search: Find evolutionary relatives faster than BLAST
  • Function prediction: "Guilt by association" — similar embedding → similar function
  • Protein design: Optimize sequences to match a target embedding
  • Variant effect prediction: Small embedding change → likely benign; large change → possibly pathogenic
  • Clustering: Group protein families without alignment

Interpreting Similarity Scores

Cosine Similarity    | Interpretation
---------------------|--------------------------------
0.90 – 1.00       | Nearly identical / same protein
0.70 – 0.90       | Close homologs, same family
0.50 – 0.70       | Distant homologs, same fold
0.30 – 0.50       | Possible remote homology
0.00 – 0.30       | Likely unrelated
< 0.00       | Opposite (very rare for proteins)

Caveat: Thresholds depend on the model, embedding dimension, and task. Always calibrate on your specific data!
