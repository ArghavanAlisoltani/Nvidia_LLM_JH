5 questions covering all three lessons | No time limit

1. A protein sequence "MALWMRLLPLL" uses one-letter codes. What does the "M" at the start represent?

Correct! Methionine (M) is encoded by the start codon AUG and is the first amino acid in almost all proteins.
Not quite. M stands for Methionine, which is encoded by the start codon AUG and initiates translation in nearly all organisms.

2. Why do protein language models use learned embeddings instead of one-hot encoding?

Correct! The key advantage is that learned embeddings capture biochemical similarity — Leucine and Isoleucine end up with similar vectors because they appear in similar contexts.
Not quite. One-hot treats all 20 amino acids as equally different. Learned embeddings capture that hydrophobic amino acids like L and I are biochemically similar.

3. What does "mean pooling" do to a protein's per-position embeddings?

Correct! Mean pooling computes the element-wise average across all residue embeddings, producing one vector representing the whole protein.
Not quite. Mean pooling averages all per-position vectors (element-wise) to create a single protein-level embedding of fixed size.

4. Two protein embeddings have a cosine similarity of 0.85. What is the most reasonable interpretation?

Correct! High cosine similarity (0.85) suggests the proteins are likely homologs — they share evolutionary ancestry and often similar structure/function. It does NOT equal sequence identity percentage.
Not quite. Cosine similarity measures embedding vector alignment, not sequence identity. 0.85 indicates likely homology, not 85% sequence identity.

5. Why is cosine similarity preferred over Euclidean distance for protein embeddings?

Correct! Cosine similarity ignores vector magnitude (scale-invariant) and remains discriminative in high-dimensional embedding spaces where Euclidean distance suffers from the "curse of dimensionality."
Not quite. The key advantages are scale invariance and better behavior in high dimensions. Magnitude is typically not biologically meaningful in normalized embeddings.
